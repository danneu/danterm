# The recycled row is filled as whole words, not one cell at a time

Research: [docs/research/39-kitten-render-benchmark](../../docs/research/39-kitten-render-benchmark/README.md)
(`H6`, `D9`, `D11`).

## 1. Problem

A whole-viewport scroll recycles the evicted row as the blank by resetting it
in place, and that reset writes the row's 179 cells one at a time: per cell a
bounds check and three stores, because the cell's two padding bytes split it
into an 8-, a 4- and a 2-byte store. It is 20% of the `ascii` feed thread and
15% of the post-`H10` `unicode` thread (`F13`, `F19`). Measured on the arms
(`D11`): a scrolled-in row is written in one third of its cells on `ascii`
and `unicode`, fully on `unique_unicode`, and `csi` never scrolls.

Load-bearing premises about existing behavior:

- A recycled row equals a freshly made blank row of the same width and style
  to every reader: cells, style, wrap claim, margin provenance, semantic
  prompt, cluster arena content (`GridRow.resetAsBlank`'s contract; the
  scroll, erase and reset suites).
- A blank painted by a background erase carries the erase style and nothing
  else, on both screens, and reads as a fill to admission
  (`TerminalCellStyleTests`, `TerminalEditingTests`, `LogicalLineStore`
  admission tests).
- `GridCell` is trivially copyable (`TerminalCellRepresentationTests`).

Desired outcome: the fill writes whole cell strides; `kitten-feed-ascii` and
`kitten-feed-unicode` read `faster`, no arm reads `slower`; nothing
observable changes.

## 2. Decision

Fill the recycled row one whole cell stride at a time in pure Swift, from a
16-byte pattern built out of the blank cell with its padding zeroed (`D11`'s
`P1c`). The
engine target keeps importing nothing: no `Darwin`, no `memset_pattern16`.
The blank-by-state ideal is recorded in `D11` and not taken.

Decisive constraints:

- The fill is correct for every style id, not only the default: the pattern
  is the cell, not zero.
- The fill accesses the cell memory only as untyped bytes. `GridCell` is
  smaller than its stride, so it is not layout compatible with any 16-byte
  word type; neither `withMemoryRebound` nor a typed `storeBytes` of a word
  is licensed over memory bound to `GridCell`. The pattern is copied in as
  raw bytes (`copyMemory`), which keeps the binding and is legal because the
  cell is trivially copyable. Alignment is therefore not a safety condition:
  no access goes through a word type.
- The fill depends on the cell's layout only through facts the code can
  check: `GridCell` is trivially copyable, and the pattern's byte count is
  exactly the cell's stride. A cell layout that breaks either fails at build
  or test time, never by writing garbage.

## 3. Invariants

- **I1** After a scroll-in, on the primary and the alternate screen, with
  and without a background-erase style pending, a never-written row reads
  as a blank row of the erase style to the cell reader, the projected
  display row, selection and search text, the inspection projection, the
  synchronization encoder round trip, and scrollback admission.
- **I2** A row that is resized after a scroll-in reads as the blank row of
  the new width.
- **I3** `memoryCensus` on a terminal that has scrolled reports the same cell
  count and the same spill accounting as before the change.
- **I4** No feed-path function of the release object copies a whole
  `Terminal` (`D5`'s gate).

## 4. Proof obligations

- **PO1 (I1)** Whole-terminal equivalence: a terminal that scrolled a row in
  and never wrote it equals one built by a fresh blank row of that style at
  that position, compared through every reader I1 names, under BCE and under
  the default style, on both screens.
- **PO2 (I2)** The resize case of PO1.
- **PO3 (I3)** The census comparison after a scrolling feed.
- **PO4 (I4)** `just test-tooling` stays green.
- **PO5** Both layout checks in section 2 -- trivial copyability and the
  pattern's byte count equal to the cell stride -- are asserted where a
  reader will see them fail (a test on the layout facts, or a build-time
  check).

## 5. Benchmark gate

[agent-docs/terminal-performance.md](../../agent-docs/terminal-performance.md).

   all four arms: `ascii` (+/-1.70%) and `unicode` (+/-1.80%) must read
   `faster`; no arm may read `slower`. `csi` (+/-1.45%) has no rotation on

## 6. Non-goals

- A row that is blank by state (short row, range row, or a shared blank
  buffer): priced and not chosen in `D11`.
- The partial-region scroll (`H1`'s open line): it does not recycle rows.
- Any change to what a blank row means to admission, projection or the sync
  encoder.

## 7. Accepted risks

- **AR1** The fill assumes a cell layout. Accepted because the assumption is
  checked (section 2, PO5) and the cell is already pinned trivially
  copyable.
- **AR2** The win lands on arms DanTerm already leads (`F22`). Accepted as
  headroom: the change is small, local, and behind a stated contract.

## 8. Rejected ideas

- `memset_pattern16`: same stores, but it puts `Darwin` into an engine target
  that imports nothing.
- `update(repeating:)` over the cell type: lowers to the same three stores
  (`D11` `P1b`, `equivalent` on both arms).
- Filling only past what the next print will cover: the scroll runs before
  the next bytes are parsed; knowing them there is lookahead across a chunk
  boundary.

## 9. Implementation discretion

- How the pattern is built and which untyped raw-copy spelling writes it,
  provided the constraints in section 2 hold.

## Implementation notes

- The pattern is a 16-byte tuple initialized to zero, with the blank cell written
  into it by `storeBytes`. `storeBytes` writes `MemoryLayout<GridCell>.size`
  bytes, so the cell's two padding bytes stay zero and the pattern never carries
  a byte nothing initialized.
- `PO5`'s two layout checks land in two places. Trivial copyability and the
  16-byte stride were already asserted by
  `TerminalCellRepresentationTests.liveGridCellShape`; the pattern's byte count
  is exposed as `Terminal.blankCellPatternByteCount` and asserted equal to
  `Terminal.gridCellStrideBytes`. A cell that outgrew the pattern also traps in
  `storeBytes`, so neither check can fail by writing garbage.
- `PO1`'s oracle is a one-row `DL` on the bottom row, which fills from
  `makeBlankRow`. The two scripts have to print the same characters in the same
  order: the content-identity counter and the synchronization encoder's
  last-printed-grapheme state both differ otherwise, for reasons that have
  nothing to do with the fill.
- Gate step 3 is met on its first half and not on its second. The per-cell store
  loop is gone -- every sample that sat on it now sits on the stride fill, and
  the emitted code is one `stp` per cell where it was a bounds check and three
  stores -- but `resetAsBlank`'s self share falls only 24.9% -> 20.6% of the
  `ascii` thread, not under the 5% the gate names. What is left is byte traffic,
  not store count, and only `D11`'s blank-by-state ideal removes it. Recorded in
  `F23`.
